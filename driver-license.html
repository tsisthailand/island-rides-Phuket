Two ways to get a Thai license

For a motorbike license, a car license, or both.

🆕 No license yet — new Thai license

If you don’t hold a driving license, a new Thai license usually involves registration, a short training session, a written test and a practical test at an authorised driving school or transport office.

Usually needed: passport, medical certificate, certificate of residence, TM30.

🔄 Licensed at home — convert to a Thai license

If you already hold a valid license from your home country, converting it to a Thai license is normally simpler.

Usually needed: passport, your original home license, medical certificate, certificate of residence, TM30. Depending on your nationality, your license may need translation and legalization at your consulate first.

Frequently asked questions

Can a foreigner get a Thai driving license?

Yes. Foreigners with the right visa and documents can apply for a Thai motorbike or car license at the Department of Land Transport, either as a new license or by converting a valid license from home.

What is the difference between a new license and a conversion?

If you don’t have a license yet, a new license usually involves training, a written test and a practical test. If you already hold a valid license from home, a conversion is normally simpler. We can tell you which route fits you.

What documents are usually needed?

Typically your passport, a medical certificate, a certificate of residence and a TM30. For a conversion you also need your original home license. Requirements can change, so message us and we’ll explain what applies to you.

Does my home license need to be translated?

Depending on your nationality, your license may need to be translated and legalized at your consulate first. Ask us and we’ll explain what to check.

Can I ride or drive with my home license or an IDP?

Rules depend on your license type and how long you stay. Message us and we’ll explain what you need to ride or drive legally in Thailand.
